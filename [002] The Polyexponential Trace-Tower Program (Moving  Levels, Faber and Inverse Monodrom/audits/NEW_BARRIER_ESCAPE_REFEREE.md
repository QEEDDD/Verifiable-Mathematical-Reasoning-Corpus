## 1. Prime-degree Morse theorem

### Verdict: PASS

Let `m>=3` be prime and write

\[
 P'_{q,m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_jX^j,
 \qquad
 d_j=m[z^{m-j-1}]A_q(z)^{j+1}.
\]

The valuations used in the proof are correct:

\[
 v_m(d_0)=-q,\qquad v_m(d_j)\ge 1\ (1\le j\le m-2),
 \qquad v_m(d_{m-1})=1.
\]

Every intermediate point is strictly above the line from `(0,-q)` to
`(m-1,1)`. Hence the derivative has one Newton edge and all critical
points have valuation

\[
 -\frac{q+1}{m-1}.
\]

After a finite ramified extension, the residual polynomial is a binomial
`u+vY^(m-1)`. It is separable because the residue characteristic is `m`
and `m` does not divide `m-1`. Thus all `m-1` critical points are simple
and have distinct leading residues.

The critical-value comparison is also correct. If `alpha` is a critical
point, `alpha^m` is the unique lowest-valuation term of `P(alpha)`. The
displayed difference for the linear term is exactly one, and all
intermediate terms are strictly higher. Since every residual root satisfies
`y^(m-1)=C`, its critical-value residue is `y^m=Cy`; distinct roots give
distinct residues. This proves the full Morse property, not merely one
transposition.

A connected polynomial cover whose finite branch points are all simple and
distinct has a transitive group generated by transpositions, hence `S_m`.
The arithmetic conclusion follows because it contains geometric `S_m`.

No hidden use of transcendence, a real ordering, or a finite computation
occurs here.

## 2. Moving-prime Newton polygon

### Verdict: PASS

Assume

\[
 \frac{m+1}{2}<p<m,\qquad r=m-p.
\]

The strict inequality gives `2<=r<=p-2` and `m<=2p-2`. Therefore a
composition contributing to the relevant coefficient can contain at most
one copy of the exceptional coefficient `a_(p-1)`. The valuation table in
the notes follows. In particular:

- `d_1` has the unique lowest contribution
  `2m a_(p-1)a_(r-1)`;
- `d_r` has the unique lowest contribution
  `(r+1)m a_(p-1)`;
- the fixed sign of all composition terms prevents cancellation in these
  two coefficients.

The resulting derivative Newton polygon really is

\[
 (0,-1)\to(1,-q-1)\to(r,-q-1)\to(m-1,0).
\]

Its three root populations and their valuations are correct. For the
one-point first edge, the derivative relation gives

\[
 P(\alpha)=\frac12d_0\alpha+\text{higher terms},
 \qquad v_p(P(\alpha))=q-1.
\]

The only integration denominator that might alter a principal face is at
degree `p`. The Frobenius calculation

\[
 [z^r]A_q(z)^p\equiv[z^r]A_q(z^p)=0\pmod p,
 \quad 0<r<p,
\]

correctly proves that this coefficient remains integral. The final-edge
critical values consequently have valuation

\[
 -\frac{m(q+1)}{p-1}<0.
\]

No missing denominator occurs because `m<2p`, so `p` is the only possible
prime divisor among the integration indices.

### Boundary warning

The strict window `p>(m+1)/2` is essential. A prime merely in `(m/2,m)`
can equal `(m+1)/2`, in which case `r=p-1` and two exceptional factors can
enter the lowest layer. The scripts enforce the strict inequality
correctly. The sentence in `ODD_TRACE_PADIC_ROUTE.md` invoking Bertrand's
theorem should not be read as proving the existence of a *strict-window*
prime in every odd degree. This is harmless for the proved theorems:
prime degrees have Theorem 2.1, and every computational witness is checked
against the strict bound.

## 3. Residual polar criterion

### Verdict: PASS

On the horizontal face, the dominant contribution to the coefficient of
`X^(j+1)` is, up to one common nonzero scalar,

\[
 C_j=[z^{r-j}]A_q(z)^j.
\]

Thus the face polynomial and its derivative are exactly

\[
 \frac{X^2}{r}R_{q,r}(X),\qquad
 \frac{X}{r}\bigl(2R_{q,r}(X)+XR'_{q,r}(X)\bigr),
\]

where `R_(q,r)=P'_(q,r) mod p`. At a nonzero unit residue `x`, simultaneous
vanishing of the principal critical value and the principal critical
equation is equivalent to

\[
 R_{q,r}(x)=R'_{q,r}(x)=0.
\]

Therefore square-freeness of `R_(q,r)` forces every horizontal-face
critical value to have valuation exactly `-(q+1)`. Together with the final
negative face and the isolated value of valuation `q-1`, the latter value
is globally unshared and has one simple critical point above it. Its inertia
is a transposition.

At this point the proof uses the previously established indecomposability
of `P_(q,m)`, hence primitivity. The standard theorem that a primitive
permutation group containing a transposition is `S_m` then applies. This
dependency is explicit and is not a gap in the present note, but the
moving-prime theorem is not logically standalone without it.

The phrase "exact obstruction" must remain first-layer language:
non-square-freeness of `R_(q,r)` means that the sufficient polar test fails.
It does **not** prove an actual characteristic-zero critical-value collision,
nor does it imply that the monodromy is alternating. The notes' final
scope statements respect this distinction.

The infinite family `m=p+2` is valid: `r=2`, so the residual derivative is
linear for every order and every odd `p>=5`. The Dirichlet subfamily
`p=1 mod 3` indeed gives infinitely many composite degrees `p+2`.

## 4. Fixed-degree large-order regular-polygon theorem

### Verdict: PASS

For fixed `m`, the composition product inequality

\[
 n_1\cdots n_k\ge m-k+1
\]

is correct, with equality exactly at permutations of
`(m-k+1,1,...,1)`. Hence

\[
 h_{q;m,k}\le [z^m](e^z-1)^k\,(m-k+1)^{-q}.
\]

After the scaling

\[
 s_q=m^{-q/(m-1)},\qquad
 \widetilde P_{q,m}(y)=m^{qm/(m-1)}P_{q,m}(s_qy),
\]

the endpoint terms are exactly

\[
 y^m+\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}y.
\]

Every intermediate coefficient is bounded by a constant times

\[
 \left(\frac{m^{(r-1)/(m-1)}}r\right)^q,
 \qquad 2\le r\le m-1.
\]

The strict inequality of this base from one follows from the monotonicity
of `log(x)/(x-1)`. Thus the coefficientwise and compact-uniform limit is
valid, with an explicit geometric error.

The limiting derivative has the simple regular-polygon roots

\[
 y^{m-1}=\frac{(-1)^m}{m!},
\]

and the corresponding values are a nonzero scalar times these distinct
vertices. The limit polynomial is therefore Morse. The product of the
derivative discriminant and the discriminant of the critical-value
resultant is a polynomial in the coefficients and is nonzero precisely on
the Morse locus. Convergence to a nonzero value proves eventual Morse.

Effectivity is justified: all coefficient error constants, polygon roots,
Rouche radii, and limiting value separations are algebraic and explicitly
computable. The theorem does not supply a convenient numerical `Q(m)`, but
"effectively computable" is mathematically warranted.

The monodromy conclusion is also sound. A Morse polynomial has simple,
distinct finite branch values; the finite inertia generators are
transpositions and generate a transitive group, necessarily `S_m`.

## 7. Reproducibility qualification

Both finite claims are supported by deterministic exact modular arithmetic,
and both full runs were reproduced. However, each script emits only
aggregate statistics rather than a durable list of every `(m,p,r)` witness.
For a publication-grade computer-assisted theorem I recommend adding:

1. a machine-readable witness file containing the selected prime and
   residual degree for every row;
2. a small independent verifier that consumes only that file;
3. hashes of both the witness file and complete stdout, in addition to the
   present source hashes.

This would reduce trust in the search implementation and make the finite
claims independently checkable in seconds. Its absence is a reproducibility
weakness, not a logical counterexample to the current exact runs.
