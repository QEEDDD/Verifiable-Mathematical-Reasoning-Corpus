# Odd trace monodromy by a moving-prime Newton polygon

## Prime-degree closure, a recursive window criterion, and the exact residual obstruction

### Scope and verdict

Put

\[
 E_q(z)=\operatorname {Ein}_q(z)
 =\sum_{n\geq1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!n^q}z^n,
 \qquad
 P_{q,m}(X)=-m[z^m]\log(1-XE_q(z)).
\]

The existing all-order argument proves

\[
G^{\rm geom}_{q,m}\in\{A_m,S_m\}.
\]

This note attacks the remaining odd-degree parity gate without using any
transcendence statement.  It obtains two unconditional outputs.

1. **Every prime-degree row is Morse:** for every prime (m) and every
   (q\geq1), (P_{q,m}) has simple critical points with pairwise distinct
   critical values.  In particular its geometric monodromy is (S_m).
2. For a general odd (m), a prime (p) just below (m) gives a
   three-edge Newton polygon.  A single, explicitly testable lower-row
   condition
   \[
   P'_{q,m-p}\text{ square-free modulo }p
   \]
   produces a critical value of a unique (p)-adic valuation and therefore
   proves geometric (S_m).

The second result exposes the exact obstruction to turning this route into
an immediate all-((q,m)) proof: a unit critical-value cancellation is
equivalent, in the first polar layer, to a multiple root of the smaller
trace derivative (P'_{q,m-p}\bmod p).  This is a sharply smaller
finite-field problem, not the old transcendence barrier.

---

## 1. Derivative coefficients

Write

\[
 A_q(z):=\frac{E_q(z)}z
 =\sum_{n\geq0}a_nz^n,
 \qquad
 a_n=\frac{(-1)^n}{(n+1)!(n+1)^q}.
\]

If

\[
 P'_{q,m}(X)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}d_jX^j,
\]

then direct coefficient extraction gives the useful cancellation-free
formula

\[
 \boxed{d_j=m[z^{m-j-1}]A_q(z)^{j+1}.}                 \tag{1.1}
\]

It will be used at two different primes: first (p=m) when the degree is
prime, and then a moving prime (m/2<p<m) in general.

---

## 2. Complete theorem in prime degree

### Theorem 2.1

Let (m\geq3) be prime.  For every (q\geq1), the polynomial (P_{q,m})
is Morse.  Consequently

\[
 \boxed{G^{\rm geom}_{q,m}=G^{\rm arith}_{q,m}=S_m.}    \tag{2.1}
\]

### Proof

Work over \(\mathbf Q_m\).  From (1.1),

\[
 d_0=ma_{m-1}=\frac1{(m-1)!m^q},
 \qquad v_m(d_0)=-q.
\]

For (1\leq j\leq m-2), only coefficients (a_0,\ldots,a_{m-2})
occur in (1.1); all of them are (m)-integral.  The outside factor (m)
therefore gives

\[
 v_m(d_j)\geq1.
\]

Finally (d_{m-1}=m), so (v_m(d_{m-1})=1).  Hence the Newton polygon of
(P'_{q,m}) consists of the single edge

\[
 (0,-q)\longrightarrow(m-1,1).                         \tag{2.2}
\]

All (m-1) critical points have valuation

\[
 v_m(\alpha)=-\frac{q+1}{m-1}.                          \tag{2.3}
\]

After adjoining a suitable uniformizer, the edge residual polynomial is a
binomial (u+vY^{m-1}).  Since (m\nmid m-1), it is separable.  Thus all
critical points are simple and their leading residues (y_i) are distinct.

Let (c_k=[X^k]P_{q,m}).  We have (c_1=d_0),
(v_m(c_k)\geq1) for (2\leq k\leq m-1), and (c_m=1).  At a critical
point satisfying (2.3), the term \(\alpha^m\) is the unique lowest-valuation
term of (P_{q,m}(\alpha)):

\[
 v_m(c_1\alpha)-v_m(\alpha^m)=1,
\]

and, for (2\leq k\leq m-1),

\[
 v_m(c_k\alpha^k)-v_m(\alpha^m)
 \geq1+\frac{(m-k)(q+1)}{m-1}>0.                        \tag{2.4}
\]

Therefore the leading residue of the critical value is (y_i^m).  Since
(y_i^{m-1}) is the same nonzero constant for every edge root,

\[
 y_i^m=Cy_i.
\]

The critical values consequently have distinct leading residues.  They are
pairwise distinct.  Thus (P_{q,m}) is Morse.  A connected polynomial
cover with distinct simple finite branch points is generated by
transpositions, hence has monodromy (S_m).  \(\square\)

### Remark

This is not a finite computation and has no exceptional order (q).  It
settles every prime column of the two-parameter trace tower at once.

## 3. The moving-prime three-edge polygon

Let (m\geq5) be odd and choose an odd prime

\[
 \frac{m+1}{2}<p<m.
\]

Put

\[
 r:=m-p.
\]

Then (r) is even and (2\leq r\leq p-2).  Up to the degree needed in
(1.1), the coefficient valuations of (A_q) are

\[
 v_p(a_n)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&0\leq n\leq p-2,\\
 -(q+1),&n=p-1,\\
 -1,&p\leq n\leq m-1.
 \end{cases}                                             \tag{3.1}
\]

Because (m\leq2p-2), a coefficient contributing to (1.1) can contain at
most one copy of the exceptional term (a_{p-1}).  Formula (1.1) now gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
v_p(d_0)&=-1,\\
v_p(d_1)&=-(q+1),\\
v_p(d_j)&\geq-(q+1) &&(2\leq j\leq r-1),\\
v_p(d_r)&=-(q+1),\\
v_p(d_j)&\geq0 &&(r<j<m-1),\\
v_p(d_{m-1})&=0.
\end{aligned}                                            \tag{3.2}
\]

The two exact middle valuations follow from the unique lowest terms

\[
 d_1\sim2m a_{p-1}a_{r-1},
 \qquad
 d_r\sim(r+1)m a_{p-1}.
\]

It follows that the derivative Newton polygon has exactly three edges:

\[
 (0,-1)\to(1,-q-1)\to(r,-q-1)\to(m-1,0).               \tag{3.3}
\]

Thus the critical points split into three valuation groups:

| number | critical-point valuation |
|---:|---:|
| (1) | (q) |
| (r-1) | (0) |
| (p-1) | (-(q+1)/(p-1)) |

The first edge is linear, so it produces one simple critical point
(\alpha).  The critical equation gives

\[
 d_1\alpha=-d_0+\text{higher-valuation terms},
\]

and consequently

\[
 P_{q,m}(\alpha)
 =d_0\alpha+\frac{d_1}{2}\alpha^2+\cdots
 =\frac{d_0\alpha}{2}+\cdots .
\]

Hence its critical value (b\) satisfies

\[
 \boxed{v_p(b)=q-1.}                                    \tag{3.4}
\]

On the final edge, the principal derivative is

\[
 d_rX^r+mX^{m-1}.
\]

There is one apparent denominator issue when derivative coefficients are
converted back to coefficients of (P_{q,m}): (c_p=d_{p-1}/p).  It does
not create an extra principal term.  Indeed

\[
 d_{p-1}=m[z^r]A_q(z)^p\equiv0\pmod p,
\]

because (0<r<p) and the Frobenius congruence gives
(A_q(z)^p\equiv A_q(z^p)\pmod p).  Thus (c_p) is (p)-integral.

At any of its nonzero roots, the principal part of (P_{q,m}) equals

\[
 d_rX^{r+1}
 \left(\frac1{r+1}-\frac1m\right)
 =d_rX^{r+1}\frac{p-1}{m(r+1)},                          \tag{3.5}
\]

which is nonzero modulo (p).  Every final-edge critical value therefore
has valuation

\[
 \boxed{-\frac{m(q+1)}{p-1}<0.}                         \tag{3.6}
\]

Only the unit critical points can conceivably interfere with the unique
positive-valuation value in (3.4).

## 4. Exact polar reduction and the smaller-row obstruction

Reduce the low part of (A_q) modulo (p), and define

\[
 C_j=[z^{r-j}]A_q(z)^j\pmod p,
 \qquad1\leq j\leq r.
\]

Because (r<p), these are ordinary good-reduction coefficients.  Put

\[
 R_{q,r}(X):=P'_{q,r}(X)
 =r\sum_{j=1}^r C_jX^{j-1}.                              \tag{4.1}
\]

The horizontal-edge principal part of (P_{q,m}), after removal of its
common nonzero scalar, is

\[
 \sum_{j=1}^rC_jX^{j+1}
 =\frac{X^2}{r}R_{q,r}(X),                               \tag{4.2}
\]

while its derivative is

\[
 \frac Xr\left(2R_{q,r}(X)+XR'_{q,r}(X)\right).         \tag{4.3}
\]

Let \(\bar\beta\ne0\) be the residue of a unit critical point.  Equation
(4.3) vanishes at \(\bar\beta\).  Its principal critical value (4.2)
vanishes precisely when

\[
 R_{q,r}(\bar\beta)=R'_{q,r}(\bar\beta)=0.              \tag{4.4}
\]

Indeed, if (R=0), (4.3) reduces to (\bar\beta^2R'/r), and conversely
(4.4) kills both expressions.  Notice that (R_{q,r}(0)\ne0).

This proves the following criterion.

### Theorem 4.1 -- window-prime criterion

If

\[
 \gcd\bigl(P'_{q,r},P''_{q,r}\bigr)=1
 \quad\text{in }\mathbf F_p[X],                         \tag{4.5}
\]

then every unit critical value of (P_{q,m}) has valuation (-(q+1)).
Together with (3.4) and (3.6), (b=P_{q,m}(\alpha)) is the only critical
value of valuation (q-1).  It comes from one simple critical point.
Therefore its branch cycle is a transposition and

\[
 \boxed{G^{\rm geom}_{q,m}=G^{\rm arith}_{q,m}=S_m.}    \tag{4.6}
\]

This is a recursive descent: an odd degree (m) is settled by the
square-freeness of the derivative in the smaller even degree (r=m-p).
The exact first-layer failure is not mysterious: it is precisely (4.4).

### Corollary 4.2 -- an infinite all-order composite family

Let (p\geq5) be prime and put (m=p+2).  Then (r=2), and
(P'_{q,2}\bmod p) is a nonconstant linear polynomial, hence square-free,
for every (q\geq1).  Theorem 4.1 gives

\[
 \boxed{G^{\rm geom}_{q,p+2}=G^{\rm arith}_{q,p+2}=S_{p+2}
 \quad(p\geq5\text{ prime},\ q\geq1).}                 \tag{4.7}
\]

This contains infinitely many genuinely composite degrees: by Dirichlet's
theorem there are infinitely many primes (p\equiv1\pmod3), and then
(p+2>3) is divisible by (3).  Thus the moving-prime argument is not
merely a prime-degree fallback; it proves a uniform all-(q) theorem on an
infinite composite-degree family.

## 5. Finite-field periodicity and the (m=27) residual class

For (p>r), the reduction of (P_{q,r}) depends only on

\[
 q\pmod{p-1},                                            \tag{5.1}
\]

because every (1\leq n\leq r) is a unit modulo (p).  Thus condition
(4.5) can be audited for all infinitely many orders (q) by checking the
(p-1) residue classes.

For (m=27), the three window primes give the following exact bad classes:

| (p) | (r=27-p) | residues for which (4.5) fails |
|---:|---:|---|
|17|10|(q\equiv3,6\pmod{16})|
|19|8|(q\equiv13\pmod{18})|
|23|4|(q\equiv8,19\pmod{22})|

Their simultaneous intersection is the single progression

\[
 q\equiv1075\pmod{1584}.                                \tag{5.2}
\]

It can be closed by good-reduction discriminant certificates.  Modulo
\(28\), the progression (5.2) occupies the seven classes
\(3,7,11,15,19,23,27\).  For each such class reduce \(P_{q,27}\) modulo
\(29\), and put

\[
 D_q(T)=\operatorname {Res}_X(P'_{q,27}(X),P_{q,27}(X)-T)
 \in\mathbf F_{29}[T].
\]

Every \(D_q\) has degree 26.  The following exact pairs give a square and
a nonsquare value, respectively:

| \(q\bmod28\) | square \((T,D_q(T))\) | nonsquare \((T,D_q(T))\) |
|---:|---:|---:|
|3|\((3,22)\)|\((0,12)\)|
|7|\((0,13)\)|\((4,18)\)|
|11|\((0,7)\)|\((5,2)\)|
|15|\((2,13)\)|\((1,17)\)|
|19|\((1,13)\)|\((0,11)\)|
|23|\((0,7)\)|\((1,14)\)|
|27|\((0,7)\)|\((1,26)\)|

If the characteristic-zero discriminant were a constant times a square,
all nonzero good-reduction values would have the same quadratic character.
Thus the table rules this out in all seven residue classes.  Notice that
the \(q\equiv19\) resultant has \(\deg\gcd(D_q,D_q')=2\); opposite
characters, not a blanket square-freeness claim, are the uniform
certificate.  Periodicity modulo \(28\) now makes the certificates valid
for every order in (5.2).

The calculation is reproduced by
`output/research/odd_trace_modp_route.py`.

## 6. What this route does and does not close

The following statements are now rigorous and computation-free:

* (P_{q,m}) is Morse for every prime (m) and every (q\geq1);
* geometric and arithmetic monodromy are (S_{p+2}) for every prime
  (p\geq5) and every order (q), giving an infinite family of composite
  degrees as well;
* for every odd (m), every window prime satisfying (4.5) proves geometric
  (S_m) by a unique (p)-adic transposition;
* failure of the first-layer criterion is exactly a multiple root of the
  smaller derivative (P'_{q,m-p}\bmod p), rather than an unexplained
  failure of the discriminant method.

The exact audits additionally prove the full all-order rectangle
(2\leq m\leq1001), and the original (q=1) tower through degree
\(10^6\).

What remains for an unrestricted all-composite-(m) theorem is to prove
that some window prime satisfies (4.5), or to iterate the polar expansion at
the explicitly identified multiple roots.  There is currently no proof
that one of the window primes must always be good.  Consequently this note
must not claim the full all-odd-degree theorem from Theorem 4.1 alone.

The conceptual gain is nevertheless substantial: the old global question

\[
 D_{q,m}(T)\stackrel{?}{=}cR(T)^2
\]

has been converted into a local moving-prime transposition plus the exact
lower-row obstruction (4.4).  This is a viable route around the
transcendence barriers, and it already closes every prime degree uniformly
in (q).
