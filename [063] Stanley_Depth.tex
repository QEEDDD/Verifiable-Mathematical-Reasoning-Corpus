\begin{abstract}
We give explicit finite interval partitions proving
$\sd(I_{14,2})=6$, $\sd(I_{17,2})=7$, and $\sd(I_{19,3})=7$ over every
field. The established variable-splitting recurrence then determines
$\sd(I_{n,2})$ for $14\le n\le19$ and $\sd(I_{n,3})$ for
$19\le n\le22$. These are ten parameter cases of one general
Stanley-depth conjecture, not ten separately resolved conjectures.
All three certificates, encoded by cyclic interval representatives,
and a complete standard-library verifier are embedded in this single
source. Independent mask-by-mask checks validate the expanded partitions.
The cases lie outside the explicit ranges of the primary papers
examined; historical priority is not established. The general conjecture
remains unresolved by this work. The proof search adapts a strategy of
handling forced low-rank objects before completing a disjoint cover;
its algebraic correspondence, counting bound, and recurrence are established
tools rather than new principles.
\end{abstract}

\section{The precise external problem and the result}
Let $S=\K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, where $\K$ is any field, and let
\[
 I_{n,d}=(x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_d}:1\le i_1<\cdots<i_d\le n).
\]
A Stanley decomposition is a direct sum of multigraded $\K$-vector
spaces $I=\bigoplus_j u_j\K[Z_j]$, with each summand free over the
polynomial ring on the variable set $Z_j$. Its depth is
$\min_j|Z_j|$, and $\sd(I)$ is the maximum of these depths.
The squarefree Veronese formula conjecture predicts
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conjecture}
 \sd(I_{n,d})=d+\left\lfloor\frac{n-d}{d+1}\right\rfloor.
\end{equation}
This is the conjecture discussed by Cimpoea\c{s} and
Keller--Shen--Streib--Young~\cite{KSSY}, and subsequently by
Ge--Lin--Shen~\cite{GLS}. It concerns Stanley depth. The known formula
for Hilbert depth does not establish it~\cite[Remark~3.11]{BCK}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
Over every field,
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
d&n&\sd(I_{n,d})\\\hline
2&14,15,16&6\\
2&17,18,19&7\\
3&19,20,21,22&7
\end{array}
\]
\end{theorem}

The result is a finite-case confirmation of \eqref{eq:conjecture}.
It is not a resolution for every $n,d$, nor a resolution of the separate
general inequality $\sd(M)\ge\operatorname{depth}(M)$, which is false
for general multigraded modules. No WOWII numbered entry is added by
this result.

\section{From interval partitions to a complete proof}
Write $P_{n,d}=\{A\subseteq[n]:|A|\ge d\}$ and
$[A,B]=\{U:A\subseteq U\subseteq B\}$.

\begin{lemma}[Classical interval correspondence]\label{lem:interval}
If $P_{n,d}$ is partitioned into intervals $[A,B]$ with $|B|\ge s$,
then $\sd(I_{n,d})\ge s$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each interval take the Stanley space
$x_A\K[x_i:i\in B]$, where $x_A=\prod_{i\in A}x_i$.
Its monomials have precisely the supports in $[A,B]$. Every monomial
of $I_{n,d}$ has a support in exactly one interval and lies in the
corresponding space. Thus these spaces form a direct sum equal to
the entire ideal, including all nonsquarefree monomials. Every space
has at least $s$ free variables.
\end{proof}
This direction of the Herzog--Vl\u{a}doiu--Zheng correspondence is
recalled explicitly in \cite[Section~2.1]{KSSY}; see also~\cite{HVZ}.

\begin{lemma}[Counting upper bound]\label{lem:upper}
For $1\le d\le n$,
\[
 \sd(I_{n,d})\le d+\left\lfloor\frac{n-d}{d+1}\right\rfloor.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
In any Stanley decomposition of depth $s$, each squarefree degree-$d$
monomial belongs to a summand whose generating monomial is exactly
that monomial: there are no smaller-degree monomials in the ideal.
At least $s-d$ of its free variables are outside its support. Multiplying
by these variables supplies at least $s-d$ distinct squarefree
degree-$(d+1)$ monomials. Directness makes all the resulting monomials
distinct between summands. Hence
\[
 \binom nd(s-d)\le\binom n{d+1}.
\]
Taking an integer upper bound proves the claim. If $s\le d$, it is
immediate.
\end{proof}
This is the established bound of \cite[Lemma~2.2]{KSSY}.

\subsection{The three explicit certificates}
Identify $[n]$ with $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ by shifting the labels by one.
An integer mask encodes a subset: bit $i$ denotes $x_{i+1}$.
For each seed $(A,B)$ in Appendix~\ref{app:code}, rotate both masks
through all $n$ cyclic shifts and keep the distinct resulting intervals.
The data have the following exact sizes.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
$(n,d)$ & Top rank & Seeds & Nontrivial intervals & All intervals & $|P_{n,d}|$\\
\midrule
$(14,2)$&6&92&1274&12911&16369\\
$(17,2)$&7&472&8024&109294&131054\\
$(19,3)$&7&969&18411&480492&524097\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For the first case the lower-rank counts are $91,455,728$ at ranks
$2,4,5$. For the second they are $136,1020,1768,5100$ at ranks
$2,4,5,6$. For the third they are $969,5814,11628$ at ranks $3,5,6$.
All nontrivial upper endpoints have the displayed target rank.

The verifier checks endpoint validity, distinctness of seed orbits,
pairwise disjointness of expanded intervals, and coverage of every
subset with rank between $d$ and the target minus one. Every still
uncovered higher-rank subset is assigned its singleton interval.
These checks therefore establish a partition of all of $P_{n,d}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:interval} proves the lower bounds $6,7,7$, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:upper} makes them equalities.

This is an exact finite proof certificate. It is not a random test of
the conjecture, and it does not require trusting an optimization
solver's success report. The full data and a checker are in the paper;
the same program is provided separately as \texttt{certificates.py}.
The certificate establishes the assertion over every field because
the support partition is independent of its coefficients.

\subsection{Propagation to ten parameter cases}
\begin{lemma}[Established variable splitting]\label{lem:recurrence}
For $n>d\ge2$,
\[
\sd(I_{n,d})\ge\min\{\sd(I_{n-1,d}),1+\sd(I_{n-1,d-1})\}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the full Herzog--Vl\u{a}doiu--Zheng correspondence~\cite{HVZ},
choose interval partitions attaining the two Stanley depths on the right.
Partition supports not containing $n$ using that partition of $P_{n-1,d}$.
Partition supports containing $n$ by adjoining $n$ to both endpoints
of each interval in a partition of $P_{n-1,d-1}$. These two families
are disjoint and cover the required supports. Their minimum top ranks
give the bound.
\end{proof}
This is the standard recurrence from \cite[Lemma~2.5]{KSSY}.
We also use the established maximal-ideal formula
$\sd(I_{m,1})=\lceil m/2\rceil$~\cite{Biro}.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
The three base values follow from the verified certificates above.
Starting with $\sd(I_{14,2})=6$, the recurrence and maximal-ideal
formula imply $\sd(I_{m,2})\ge6$ for every $m\ge14$.
Starting with $\sd(I_{17,2})=7$ gives $\sd(I_{m,2})\ge7$ for every
$m\ge17$. The upper bound equals six at $14\le n\le16$ and seven
at $17\le n\le19$, proving the first two rows.
Finally start with $\sd(I_{19,3})=7$. The $d=3$ recurrence needs only
$\sd(I_{m,2})\ge6$, already established for all $m\ge14$.
It therefore gives $\sd(I_{n,3})\ge7$ for every $n\ge19$.
The upper bound is seven at $19\le n\le22$, proving the last row.
\end{proof}

\section{What the transfer contributes}
The earlier WOWII work used forced extremal cases, selected low-degree
configurations, and disjoint certificates to turn local constraints into
global bounds. Here the analogous objects are the bottom ranks of a
Boolean lattice. At the boundary cases $(14,2),(17,2),(19,3)$, attaining
the counting upper bound exhausts the next rank exactly. We first
enforce this saturation, then cover the remaining lower ranks by disjoint
intervals. Cyclic symmetry reduces the search to interval orbits.
An exact-cover solver found the witnesses; the delivered checker has
no solver dependency.

This is an adaptation of a proof-search strategy to commutative algebra.
The graph domination inequalities do not themselves imply the Stanley-depth
result. The algebraic bridge, interval-partition method, symmetry reduction,
counting bound and recurrence have antecedents; no claim of a wholly new
method is made. The concrete output is the three explicit partitions and
their ten-case consequence.

\subsection{Verification and precise priority scope}
The embedded checker exhaustively validates all $2^{14}$, $2^{17}$ and
$2^{19}$ masks. Independent code, without cyclic-orbit logic, checks each
Boolean mask against the full expanded intervals and its singleton entry.
The two smaller cases were independently checked in Python; the largest
was checked in C++20 with integer bit operations. All three checks passed.
The research archive includes these programs, expanded and compressed
certificates, and their outputs. No formal proof-assistant verification
or external refereeing is claimed.

Keller et al. prove \eqref{eq:conjecture} for $n<5d+4$ and report
computational confirmation through $n=13$~\cite{KSSY}.
The exact range in Ge--Lin--Shen~\cite{GLS} ends at $n=10$ for $d=2$
and $n=14$ for $d=3$, and their further lower bounds do not give the
new displayed equalities. The September 2025 version of
B\u{a}l\u{a}nescu--Cimpoea\c{s}--Krattenthaler~\cite[Remark~3.11]{BCK}
retains the general Stanley-depth formula as a conjecture and explicitly
distinguishes it from the proved Hilbert-depth formula.
All ten cases are outside those explicit exact ranges. Targeted searches
located no earlier exact proof of the three base cases, but cannot rule
out every published or unpublished computation. Consequently historical
priority is left open.

\section{Other attempted transfers and what remains}
Parallel attempts considered the union-closed sets conjecture,
the $1/3$--$2/3$ order conjecture, geometric Steiner-ratio problems,
and fair-allocation and discrepancy problems. None is claimed solved
here. The accompanying research notes give exact obstructions to some
natural transfers: inclusion-preserving matchings need not exist even
when every element is abundant in a union-closed family; deleting a
simplicial incomparable element need not preserve a chosen balanced pair;
and an incidence-graph maximum cut can have arbitrarily bad discrepancy.
These are limitations of particular arguments, not counterexamples to
the named conjectures. A sharp continuous metric-tree inequality and a
shape-stability proof are also retained in the notes, with no priority claim.

The positive conclusion of this paper is precisely Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.
The general squarefree Veronese conjecture, including all larger parameter
values not covered by the cited theorems or the stated recurrence bounds,
is not settled.

def verify(n, d, target, seeds):
    full = (1 << n) - 1
    def rotate(mask, shift):
        return ((mask << shift) | (mask >> (n-shift))) & full
    intervals = set()
    distinct_orbits = set()
    for a, b in seeds:
        require(0 <= a <= full and 0 <= b <= full, "mask range")
        require(a & b == a and a.bit_count() >= d, "lower endpoint")
        require(b.bit_count() == target, "upper endpoint")
        orbit = frozenset((rotate(a,t), rotate(b,t)) for t in range(n))
        require(orbit not in distinct_orbits, "duplicate orbit")
        distinct_orbits.add(orbit)
        intervals.update(orbit)
    multiplicity = bytearray(1 << n)
    for a, b in intervals:
        free, part = b ^ a, b ^ a
        while True:
            mask = a | part
            require(multiplicity[mask] == 0, "overlapping intervals")
            multiplicity[mask] = 1
            if part == 0:
                break
            part = (part-1) & free
    singletons = 0
    for mask in range(1 << n):
        rank = mask.bit_count()
        if rank < d:
            require(multiplicity[mask] == 0, "covered outside ideal")
        elif rank < target:
            require(multiplicity[mask] == 1, "uncovered small set")
        elif multiplicity[mask] == 0:
            multiplicity[mask] = 1
            singletons += 1
    expected = sum(comb(n,j) for j in range(d,n+1))
    require(sum(multiplicity) == expected, "wrong domain size")
    require(target == d + (n-d)//(d+1), "counting upper bound")
    return dict(n=n, d=d, sdepth=target, result="PASS",
                orbit_seeds=len(seeds),
                nontrivial_intervals=len(intervals),
                singleton_intervals=singletons,
                ideal_subsets=expected,
                lower_ranks=dict(sorted(Counter(
                    a.bit_count() for a,b in intervals).items())))
